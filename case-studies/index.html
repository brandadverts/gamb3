A new operation
is a solvable problem.

New launches, controlled migrations and multi-brand growth. Three representative scenarios.

01

From zero to the first live day.

The need

A team that has finished its licensing and operational groundwork, now getting to market with every product run from one place.

The approach

The core product scope is agreed first. Brand and environment setup runs in parallel with the integration work.

Intended outcome

Validated core workflows, a trained operations team and a launch checklist both sides signed off.

  1. Scope
  2. Build
  3. Integration
  4. Rehearsal
  5. Launch
02

Same business, new foundations.

The need

Replacing the technical platform without interrupting a running operation.

The approach

Data, wallet and product flows are split into controlled phases. Each phase moves with a rollback plan attached.

Intended outcome

A migration plan you can measure and a platform you can keep building on.

  1. Inventory
  2. Data rehearsal
  3. Parallel run
  4. Traffic ramp
  5. Cut-over
03

A second brand on the same backbone.

The need

A single-brand operator opening a second brand for a new market, with its own team and its own rules.

The approach

The shared wallet and data layer stay as they are. Brand, market restrictions and permissions are configured separately.

Intended outcome

Shared infrastructure, a distinct brand identity and reporting that shows both brands together.

  1. Market rules
  2. Brand structure
  3. Content
  4. Permissions
  5. Launch

The scenarios are illustrative; they do not describe a specific customer or outcome.

Every project starts with the same four questions.

Success criteria are defined up front; progress is tracked against finished work, not assumptions.

01

Where are we going?

Target market, product scope and commercial priorities.

02

What are we protecting?

The current player experience, the data and the critical flows.

03

What are we changing?

Platform, providers, processes and team responsibilities.

04

How do we verify it?

Test scenarios, acceptance criteria and go-live measurements.

We talk about what can go wrong before it does.

The four risks that come up most often in a migration, and where the plan stands on each.

RiskCountermeasure
Data loss or drift during migrationRepeated data rehearsals before go-live and record-level comparison.
Wallet balances that do not matchBalance reconciliation at cut-over; traffic does not open until the gap is closed.
Downtime on launch dayA traffic ramp, monitoring boards and rollback steps written in advance.
The team not settling into new toolsRole-based training and a rehearsal on real scenarios before launch.

A visible plan. Clear ownership. A controlled go-live.
