"What about Andorra?" has come up in a lot of our calls lately. The short answer: today you
**cannot** get an online gambling licence in Andorra. The longer answer is more useful,
because the door is closed but you can already see where it is.

## What the law says

Andorra rewrote its framework with **Law 14/2024 of 7 November 2024 on games of chance**,
in force since December 2024. It prohibits offering gambling without a licence, including
from abroad. Licences are granted and supervised by the **CRAJ** (Consell Regulador Andorrà
del Joc).

The law also defines an online-only category. Digital licences are labelled **C@** and
**D@**; D@ covers online casino, gaming machines, bingo and fixed-odds betting.

## Why nobody can apply

The regulation that puts this part of the law into practice has not been adopted. That is
where the details will live: what operators must prove, what guarantees they deposit, how
age and identity are checked, and which technical audits a platform has to pass. In August
2026 the Andorran press reported that no approval date had been announced. The CRAJ's
director has called online gaming the body's main challenge and said regulation would come
"sooner or later — I think soon."

So: the CRAJ publishes application forms for land-based licences. There is none for D@.

## What to expect when it opens

The regulation will settle the details, but the law and the industry's reading of it point
to the outline:

- **A local company.** The holder is expected to be an Andorran public limited company with
  its registered office in Andorra and gambling as its sole object.
- **A tender.** Licences are expected to be awarded by public tender on Government-approved
  terms, not on open application.
- **A local footprint.** Requirements under discussion include an Andorran domain, real-time
  data held in Andorra and certification by an independent body.
- **Player protection.** Identity, age and address checks, a single payment account, no
  gambling on credit, deposit and stake limits, and a link to the CRAJ self-exclusion
  register.

Some industry summaries already quote application fees, annual fees and a tax rate. Use them
for planning, not for decisions — check any figure with the [CRAJ](https://www.craj.ad) and
the [official text of the law](https://portaljuridicandorra.ad/L2024014) before it goes into
a budget.

## What you can do now

Preparing for a licence that does not exist yet sounds odd, but once the regulation lands
the slow work will be technical anyway:

1. **Keep your data movable.** Can you enter a market that wants player, wallet and audit
   data held in-country without rebuilding your architecture?
2. **Make verification market-configurable.** Which document is asked for, and when, should
   be a rule, not code.
3. **Have responsible-gambling tools ready.** Limits, cool-off, self-exclusion and the
   ability to query an external register.
4. **Prepare for reports, not templates.** Nobody knows the report formats yet. With an
   immutable audit log you can produce whatever is asked for.

## The point

Andorra is a small market. For most operators it is not a target on its own but part of a
multi-market plan. The real question is whether your platform will treat the day the door
opens as a configuration task or as a development project.
